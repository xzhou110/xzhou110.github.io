Key DecisionTreat an unknown vehicle feature as unknown, not as absent.

The Case Study

Problem
During my search for a used hybrid SUV, listings were scattered across five sites and described features inconsistently. I needed to compare ownership costs, track missing information, and keep accident or rental-fleet concerns visible while working toward a deadline.
What I Built
A used-car shortlist with feature tracking, risk flags, estimated ownership costs, comparison, and Google Sheets export. Listings are captured with an assistant into a typed seed catalog; the app does not scrape listing sites.
How It Works
A pure domain engine handles the risk rules, ranking, and export. The ownership-cost engine is reused from Car TCO Compare. Browser storage holds the working shortlist, and an Apps Script endpoint supports Sheets export.
Where It Stands
Built for my own car search and available as a public, single-user web app. It applies the calculation engine from Car TCO Compare to a working shortlist.
Evidence and Limits
  • Review found an unused URL-import feature could replace saved work. I removed it; regression tests verify that crafted URLs cannot change saved data.
  • The public decision record explains the architecture and trade-offs.
  • Cost figures are estimates. Risk flags surface information to investigate; they do not verify a vehicle’s history.
